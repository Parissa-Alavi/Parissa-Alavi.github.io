About

Mechanics, materials and code — in the same workflow.

I am a mechanical and materials engineer with a PhD from EPFL, where my doctoral research was carried out in industrial collaboration with Novelis Switzerland.

My work sits at the intersection of nonlinear mechanics, surface and contact phenomena, metal forming, microstructure evolution, numerical methods and scientific software. I enjoy taking a physical problem from formulation to implementation, validation and engineering interpretation.

Across research projects I have developed multiscale models, custom numerical solvers, automated simulation workflows and data-driven surrogates using Python, C++, MATLAB, Abaqus and related tools.

Selected projects

From microscale mechanisms to engineering predictions.

A selection of work spanning tribology, multiscale mechanics, metal forming, microstructural modelling, electrochemical device design and scientific software.

01 Tribology · Multiscale mechanics

Multiscale friction with third-body wear particles

Developed a hierarchical framework linking wear-particle mechanics at rough interfaces to macroscopic friction in strip drawing, coupling BEM–DEM mesoscale simulations with a finite-element representation of the forming process.

02 Contact mechanics · Lubrication

Rough contacts with trapped compressible lubricant pockets

Built a Fourier-space BEM model for partially lubricated rough contacts, coupling compressible trapped lubricant with plastic surface response to explain pressure- and lubricant-dependent friction trends.

03 Surface evolution · Numerical methods

History-dependent surface roughness evolution

Developed an efficient FFT-BEM framework with saturation-based plasticity to preserve deformation history during sliding and predict the evolution of rough metallic surfaces.

04 Computational materials · AI

FE–cellular automata–neural network microstructure modelling

Coupled finite-element deformation fields, cellular automata and neural-network surrogates to model recrystallization and martensite reversion in cold-rolled 304L stainless steel during non-isothermal annealing.

05 Electrochemical design · Ongoing

Multiphysics design of a hydrogel-based sensing device

Developing computational models of electrical conduction, diffusion and ion migration to support the design of an electrochemical sensing device. Comparing electrode contact geometries and hydrogel properties to investigate the trade-off between passive leakage and electrically driven transport.

Automating parametric studies and post-processing to assess contact area, current distribution and species transfer, and translate simulation results into design choices.
