About

Mechanics, materials, and code in the same workflow

I am a mechanical and materials engineer with a PhD from EPFL. My research focuses on predictive computational models for nonlinear, multiscale, and history-dependent physical systems.

During my doctoral work with Novelis Switzerland, I developed and validated multiscale models for friction, lubrication, surface evolution, and third-body wear in aluminium sheet-metal forming. I combined Boundary Element, Discrete Element, and Finite Element methods with experimental surface data and custom scientific software.

My earlier computational-materials work combined finite elements, cellular automata, and neural networks to predict microstructure evolution in stainless steel. I am especially interested in advanced modelling, scientific software, and AI-assisted engineering workflows.

Tribology · Multiscale Mechanics

Multiscale friction with third-body wear particles

Developed a predictive framework for friction in industrial sheet-metal forming by linking rough-contact mechanics and wear-particle dynamics to the macroscopic forming process.

  • Coupled BEM–DEM–FEM modelling
  • Third-body particle generation and transport
  • Industrial validation with Novelis Switzerland

Impact: Connected microscale wear-particle mechanisms to macroscopic friction trends in an industrial forming context.

Contact Mechanics · Lubrication

Rough contacts with trapped lubricant pockets

Modelled partially lubricated rough interfaces with compressible trapped liquid pockets to understand how pressure, roughness, and lubricant distribution influence contact and friction.

  • FFT-based Boundary Element Method
  • Compressible liquid pockets
  • Plastic rough-surface response

Impact: Provided a mechanistic explanation for lubricant- and pressure-dependent friction in rough metallic contacts.

Computational Materials · Neural Networks

FE–Cellular Automata–Neural Network Microstructure Modelling

Developed a coupled framework for cold-rolled 304L stainless steel linking finite-element deformation fields, cellular automata, and a neural network to predict martensite reversion, recrystallization, and evolving microstructure during non-isothermal annealing.

  • Abaqus/Explicit thermo-mechanical rolling simulation
  • Cellular automata for recrystallization and grain evolution
  • Neural network for martensite-reversion prediction
  • Experimental validation using XRD, Feritscope, metallography, hardness and tensile data

Impact: Combined physics-based and data-driven models in one validated framework for phase transformation and grain evolution.

Numerical Methods · Surface Evolution

History-dependent surface roughness evolution

Developed an efficient contact algorithm that preserves deformation history during sliding, allowing evolving metallic roughness to be simulated without relying on a memoryless plastic cap.

  • FFT-BEM contact solver
  • Saturation-based plasticity
  • Experiment–simulation comparison

Impact: Enabled rough-surface evolution to retain plastic deformation history and reproduce measured topographic trends.
